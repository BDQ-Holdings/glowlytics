## Detailed Comparison of Niacinamide's Mechanism of Action in Skincare Versus Oral Care

### Skincare Benefits

In skincare, niacinamide is renowned for its ability to improve skin barrier function, reduce inflammation, and even out skin tone by inhibiting melanin transfer. This makes it effective for treating hyperpigmentation and enhancing skin radiance. These benefits are well-documented in numerous dermatological studies, which highlight its role in reducing acne and improving skin texture.

### Oral Care Application

Translating these benefits to oral care, however, presents challenges. While niacinamide’s anti-inflammatory properties could theoretically benefit gum health, its primary skincare mechanism—melanin inhibition—does not directly apply to teeth whitening. Teeth stains are not caused by melanin but by external factors like food, drink, and tobacco, as well as internal factors such as enamel thinning. Therefore, while niacinamide may contribute to oral health in other ways, its role in whitening is not analogous to its skincare applications.

## Long-term Effects of Using Niacinamide Toothpaste on Dental Health
