### Understanding Collagen and Its Role in Skin Health

Collagen is the most abundant protein in the human body, forming the scaffold that supports skin strength and elasticity. It acts like a glue that holds everything together, ensuring our skin remains firm and supple. As we age, however, natural collagen production begins to decline, leading to the visible signs of aging such as wrinkles and sagging skin. According to [Vogue](https://www.vogue.co.uk/beauty/article/best-collagen-supplements), collagen is crucial for maintaining skin elasticity and strength, making it a focal point in skincare.

### Why Collagen Production Declines and How to Combat It

Collagen production typically starts to wane in our mid-twenties and accelerates during perimenopause due to decreasing estrogen levels. This decline is a natural part of aging, but it can be exacerbated by lifestyle factors such as sun exposure, smoking, and poor nutrition. As noted by [Good Housekeeping](https://www.goodhousekeeping.com/uk/beauty/skincare/g43138026/best-collagen-supplements/), understanding this decline is essential for combating its effects.

To combat collagen loss, many turn to supplements. Hydrolysed collagen, which is broken down into smaller peptides for better absorption, is often recommended for its bioavailability and efficacy. This form of collagen can be more easily utilized by the body, potentially improving skin elasticity and reducing wrinkles.

### Choosing the Right Collagen Supplement: Marine vs. Bovine

When it comes to collagen supplements, marine and bovine sources are the most common. Each has its unique benefits, and choosing the right one depends on your specific needs.

#### Marine Collagen

Marine collagen, derived from fish, is typically type I collagen, which is the most abundant type in the human body. It is often praised for its superior absorption rate and is particularly beneficial for skin, hair, and nails. According to [Holland & Barrett](https://www.hollandandbarrett.com/the-health-hub/vitamins-and-supplements/supplements/collagen/guide-collagen-types/), marine collagen is associated with supporting skin elasticity and hydration.

#### Bovine Collagen

Bovine collagen, sourced from cows, contains both type I and type III collagen. This combination is beneficial for not only skin health but also for joint support. Bovine collagen is often more affordable than marine collagen and is a popular choice for those seeking a more comprehensive approach to collagen supplementation.

Collagen's role in skin elasticity is supported by numerous studies. It provides structural support to the dermis, the layer of skin responsible for elasticity and strength. Supplementing with collagen can help replenish these fibers, restoring some of the skin's youthful properties.

### Exploring Other Types of Collagen
