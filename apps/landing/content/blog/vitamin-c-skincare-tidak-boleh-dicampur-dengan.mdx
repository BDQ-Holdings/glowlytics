## The Science of pH Balance in Skincare

### Vitamin C and AHA/BHA

Vitamin C should not be mixed with Alpha Hydroxy Acids (AHA) or Beta Hydroxy Acids (BHA) due to potential pH imbalance. Vitamin C, typically in the form of ascorbic acid, works best at a low pH level. AHAs and BHAs, which are also acidic, can alter the pH balance, reducing the effectiveness of Vitamin C. According to sources such as Avoskin Beauty and Halodoc, this combination can lead to decreased absorption and efficacy of Vitamin C.

### Vitamin C and Retinol

Mixing Vitamin C with Retinol can cause skin irritation. Retinol, a form of Vitamin A, requires a higher pH to be stable and effective, whereas Vitamin C needs a lower pH. When used together, the differing pH requirements can lead to irritation and decreased effectiveness of both ingredients. This interaction is well-documented by sources like Zalora and Garnier.

### Vitamin C and Benzoyl Peroxide

Vitamin C and Benzoyl Peroxide should not be used together as they can oxidize each other, reducing their effectiveness. Benzoyl Peroxide is a strong oxidizing agent used to treat acne, and when combined with Vitamin C, it can lead to the degradation of both compounds. This interaction is supported by evidence from Avoskin Beauty and Halodoc.

### Vitamin C and Peptides

Peptides and Vitamin C should not be mixed as they can interfere with each other's stability and effectiveness. ## Guidelines on Layering Skincare Products
